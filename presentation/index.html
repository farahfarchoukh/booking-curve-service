Booking Curve Service — Design & Engineering Overview

One booking-curve service for eight hotels that share almost nothing.

Five currencies, two PMS schemas, room types with as few as one physical unit, and a training window that ends the month before the season it has to forecast. Here's how the model is shaped, why, and — just as importantly — where I know it still breaks.

*hotel_C publishes an average-of-rate-plans price where the other seven publish the lowest — not comparable without knowing which.

Two more things the raw tables don't advertise. First, room-type inventories run from 1 to 17 rooms — a room type with one unit has an occupancy fraction that can only ever be 0 or 1, which makes "predict a smooth curve" the wrong mental model for close to half of hotel_C's room types. Second, I found that reservations.csv references four room_type_codes that don't exist in room_types.csv — 655 and 536 reservations on two of them, on hotel_C alone. Left unpatched, that silently drops ~24% of the real test curves from evaluation. I treat it as the kind of PMS dimension-table drift a production pipeline needs to catch, not paper over — src/data.py detects and repairs it, loudly.

02

A backbone that never sees hotel_id, plus a correction that knows exactly how much to trust it.

The model only ever sees attributes that exist for any hotel before its first reservation lands — country, currency, PMS type, rate mode, region type, room-count scale. That's what lets it say something for hotel_A tomorrow, not just hotel_C today. Tenant identity re-enters through a separate, explicitly-shrunk correction, not through a tree split on hotel_id == hotel_C.

Partial pooling without a hierarchical fit

With two fitted hotels there's nothing to estimate a group-level variance from, so instead of a full hierarchical model I use its behavior: an empirical-Bayes correction, shrunk toward zero as n/(n+K). It resolves cold start and per-hotel pooling with one mechanism:

What didn't make it into training, and why

Per-checkpoint MAE — the honest divergence

hotel_C behaves the way a booking-curve model should: error falls as the stay approaches and more is known. hotel_H runs backwards — because its Jul–Sep busy season never appears in its own Apr–Jun training data, the gap between an under-anchored forecast and real late-arriving demand widens the closer we get.

04

Cold start, and the interval that tells the truth about it.

hotel_H is a mountain resort whose busy season is entirely unseen in its own training window — and no other resort-mountain property with reservation history exists to borrow a season shape from. Rather than mask that, I measured exactly how it shows up in the prediction intervals.

P10 / P90 coverage, test window

39%
54%
below p10 — 7% within interval — 39% above p90 — 54%
Target for an 80% interval: ~80% within, ~10% on each side. In-distribution (train-window, out-of-fold): 90%.
Reported, not tuned away: conformal calibration gets in-distribution coverage to 90% — the machinery works. On the true Jul–Sep window it drops to 39%, one-sided, because the bias is in the median (under-anchored on an unseen season), and a symmetric interval can't fully correct a biased center. I widened intervals for out-of-season dates and it helped only marginally — confirming the diagnosis rather than fixing it. The real fix is structural: a seasonal-calendar feature, and conformal calibration pooled across same-region_type hotels once more exist.
05

What I'd build next, in order

  • A season_calendar reference table (peak/shoulder/low by month, from RM ops or a comp-set history) — the single highest-leverage fix for the hotel_H-shaped cold-start gap, and the one piece of context this dataset deliberately withholds.
  • Cross-hotel-family conformal calibration — pool residuals across hotels sharing a region_type once there are enough of them, so a resort-mountain property's intervals stop leaning on a resort-coastal hotel's calibration.
  • An off-policy read on suggested_prices as the historical action — the stretch goal I didn't get to, gated behind more validation-window data than this extract provides.
  • Switchback A/B against expected_booking_curves in production, hotel as a random effect — laid out in full in DESIGN.md §6.6.
